The Wayback Machine keeps an index of every file it ever saved from a site, including files no page linked to: drafts, logs, notes, meeting minutes, dumps people forgot were public. DIGSITE pulls files from that index, reads the first 32 KB of each one, and ranks them by how much secrecy, planning, cover-up or confession language they contain. The matching passages are highlighted.

HOW TO HUNT

  1. Pick a target. GeoCities and the free hosts drop you in a random corner each hunt. Named sites search the whole site.
  2. Pick which files to pull. Leaky files goes after paths named private, minutes, logs, drafts, notes, members and the like.
  3. Pick the signals to flag, and add your own words.
  4. Hit HUNT. It reads the 20 most promising files, then you can read 20 more at a time.
  5. READ opens the whole file with every hit highlighted. SAVE puts it in your case files with a note.

READING THE SCORE

  • STRONG: several distinct phrases, usually across more than one theme.
  • MEDIUM: a couple of real phrases.
  • WEAK: single common words like "classified" or "recruit". Usually noise.
  • Phrases count more than single words. Your own words count extra.

REALITY CHECK

Most hits are fiction, role-play, fan pages and kids' "secret club" pages. A hit is a lead, not proof. Check the date, read the whole file, look at the other files saved in the same folder, and open other captures in the Wayback Machine.

READ, DON'T TRACK

This tool reads what was published. It doesn't identify or locate the people behind pages, and it's not meant for that. Many authors were teenagers then. If something looks like a real crime, take it to the people whose job that is.
